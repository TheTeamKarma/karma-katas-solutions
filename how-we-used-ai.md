# How We Used AI to Produce This Entry

This is an AI-assisted architecture kata, so we used AI as an assistant throughout, while keeping every decision with a person.

| Activity | How AI helped | Human role |
|---|---|---|
| Understanding the brief | Summarised the kata slides and event page; checked our summary against the original PDF | Confirmed the reading and the open questions |
| Learning from past entries | Scanned four finalist repositories from past katas for structure, decision records and patterns judges rewarded | Chose which lessons applied |
| Exploring options | Listed options with pros and cons for each decision (e.g. device network, internet backup, forecasting method, AI gateway) and compared our AI gateway with production gateways | Challenged options, asked for alternatives, made every decision |
| Scoring AI ideas | Scored about 20 AI ideas against weighted criteria | Re-weighted scores, questioned results, picked the four AI uses |
| Checking industry practice | Web search on attraction and hotel demand forecasting | Accepted the approach after review |
| Independent review | A separate AI reviewer (a different session at maximum reasoning effort) challenged our decisions against the brief and judging criteria and reported findings by severity | Decided which findings to act on; most were accepted, some rejected with reasons |
| Team input | Compared a team member's architecture document with the brief and our design, listing its strengths and gaps | Chose what to adopt, in our own style: the management insight assistant, example technologies, the intelligence pipeline view and the one-sentence pitch |
| Drafting | Drafted the documents, ADRs and Mermaid diagrams | Reviewed and changed each stage before it was accepted |
| Diagram quality | Rendered every diagram and checked it for readability before saving | Rejected unreadable versions and asked for redraws |

## What we learned

| Lesson | Detail |
|---|---|
| AI is good at breadth | Options, comparisons and consistency checks across many documents were fast |
| People must own decisions | Several AI suggestions were changed or rejected: buying staff scheduling software, gender in pricing, fixed quotas for the box office, a 14-day forecast horizon |
| Independent review pays off | The separate reviewer found real gaps: missing connectivity decisions, no offline alarm path, no validation design |
| Check the output, not the claim | Diagrams that looked fine as text were unreadable when rendered; every diagram is now render-checked |
